Kitchen Quest

A VR cooking game that lets kids ages 6–12 practice real kitchen skills in a safe, playful space, with a parent app that brings families into the learning.

Overview

Kitchen Quest is a VR game app that teaches children kitchen skills and builds their interest in cooking, without the risks of a real kitchen. It centers on two games. I Am The Chef guides kids through cooking step by step in virtual practice, and What's In The Kitchen lets them discover kitchen tools and what each one does through hands-on interaction. Around the games, a kid-friendly community and avatar customization keep children coming back.

My focus

The project asks how game-based interaction can support kitchen education for children ages 6–12, and how parents can take an active part in that learning. I designed the interface in Figma and built a VR game prototype in Unity, creating a low-risk environment where kids can practice basic kitchen concepts and procedures.

> User Journey: Finding the Problem

Children are often eager to try cooking, but that excitement fades quickly once they meet the kitchen's real risks. In one observation, an eight-year-old participant's enthusiasm turned to anxiety as she faced unfamiliar steps and hazards like open flames and hot oil. She later said splattering oil scared her, and her curiosity became reluctance.

This reflects a broader developmental pattern. Without scaffolding, children tend to avoid environments that feel unfamiliar or dangerous, and early negative experiences can erode their confidence and motivation to keep learning.

> Pain Points

  1. Independence raises the stakes. Children's curiosity and drive to do things themselves make kitchen safety education essential.
  2. Fear overrides interest. Even children who want to cook struggle to overcome an instinctive fear of the kitchen's unfamiliar and dangerous parts.
  3. Traditional teaching dulls interest. Conventional kitchen education often turns cooking into rules and warnings, which weakens children's motivation.

How might we make learning kitchen skills fun for children, and invite parents to take part?

Four principles guided every step: learning through play, risk-free practice, an immersive kitchen, and parents who join in.

I Am The Chef Game

A virtual cooking game that teaches kids to cook through step-by-step recipe practice in a safe environment. Kids choose from a range of dishes, starting with basic recipes and unlocking more advanced cuisines as they master foundational skills. By replacing real kitchen risks with virtual practice, the game keeps children safe, while progressive levels and new unlocks keep them motivated.

What's In The Kitchen Game

This game introduces essential kitchenware, with a focus on using each tool safely and correctly. In a playful, cartoon-style virtual kitchen, children explore utensils and learn what they do through guided interaction. Hovering over the oven, for example, shows a short introduction; clicking it brings in an avatar guide who walks the child from basic concepts to correct oven use, step by step.

Avatar Dream Wardrobe

Avocadoo, Pankie, Firy, and Eggie are friendly guides who stay with children throughout the experience. Completing learning tasks or sharing progress in the community unlocks new outfits for them, turning progress into a visible reward and building a sense of achievement through play.

> Why Involve Parents

Kitchen learning ultimately happens at home, where parents set the safety rules, routines, and real-world practice. Their involvement helps carry skills from the virtual kitchen into everyday cooking, gives children emotional support and guidance, and keeps their use of immersive technology appropriately supervised.

> How Parents Engage

Education

The mobile app connects to the VR headset, so parents can guide their child's kitchen learning and follow their progress.

Community

Children share achievements on the community platform, where parents can cheer them on and send gifts to keep them motivated.

Management

Parents can see play time and progress in the app, making it easier to supervise screen use.

Reward

Parents can help children earn achievements and rewards, such as new outfits for their avatars.
